- **Typing under a chord or sequence `when:`.** A `sequence` or `chord` evaluates its `when:` before matching, on every first key-down (`mod.rs:860`, :892). So a `TitleContains` on one of them allocates on every typed key. Moving `when:` after the match for them belongs to 3.5.
- **The adapter, outside `on_key` (not A4's subject).**
  - `HookEnv` keeps the title as an `OnceCell<Option<String>>` (`keyboard_hook_callback.rs:182-185`, :213-217). `window_title` makes it with `String::from_utf16_lossy` (`src/platform/foreground.rs:169-177`): one `String` per key-down that asks for a title. This is 3.5's topic.

- **What does not allocate:**
  - `Decision::default()`, whose `Vec`s are empty;
  - `winner::elect`;
  - `matching`, `keys` and `resync`.

  So a key-down that fires and arms nothing allocates nothing, unless it evaluates a title condition or a non-ASCII condition. A1's cut prescribed exactly that (D11, `<orch>/p0-A1-engine/cut.md:217-221`).

## 4. Latency (key-to-action path)

### Lane C (groundwork)

- **What runs on the key path.** The pump runs every keyboard callback on main: the low-level hook call is a sent message, dispatched inside `PeekMessageW` in `pump` (`main_thread.rs:1012`).
- **Intended change:** none to that path.
  - The same calls, in the same order.
  - The same wake mask and flags (`QS_ALLINPUT`, `MWMO_INPUTAVAILABLE`, `main_thread.rs:815-818`).
  - The same early return at `MAIN_WAKE` (:1016-1018).
  - No allocation, lock or log; the helper is `#[inline]`.
- **Timer resolution and wake-ups:** unchanged.
- **Bench, P = parent, H = HEAD:**
  - Rows that exercise the loops: R7, R13 (round trips through `MainThread::run`), R8 (delivery under load), R14 and R14c, R15 and R16. Each must be within its rule (CLAUDE.md: D10, D14, the 2.4 rules).
  - R1 to R6 and R9 to R12 do not go through the pump. They are run and must be unchanged.
  - The property scale-up and the purity scan are test-only.

### Lane A (compiled bindings)

- **Per key-down:** the election and the blocker pass go from O(all bindings) to O(the bindings whose trigger keys accept vk), one `by_vk[vk]` lookup. Sequences and chords keep their own list; they must see every key-down, and 3.5 moves their `when:`.
- **No new cost:**
  - still one `ArcSwap` load per event (`keyboard_hook_callback.rs:260`);
  - no new Win32 call, lock or allocation;
  - the timers are unchanged.
- **Reload:** the compile (sort, index, groups, diagnostics) runs on the loading thread. Main only swaps and resets (`MainState::reload`), so main does less work during a reload than today's sort in `update_bindings` (`keyboard_hook.rs:88`).
- **With the default config of 3 bindings** the gain is near zero. R1 has a mean of 0.083 µs and R12 a p50 of 0.2 µs (`p2-2.5-latency-trim/results.md:103-108`).
- **A winner lock (if accepted):** one per-key slot write at a first key-down and one read at a repeat. A repeat then skips the election.
- **New bench row R19 (diagnostic):** 50 bindings mixing `keys` combos with and without modifiers, gesture groups, sequences, chords and a blocker.
  - Timed: `Engine::on_key` of an unbound key-down and of a bound key-down, and the same through the callback.
  - Run on P and H. Expected H ≤ P.
  - It is also the row 3.5 needs for its "100 µs with 50 bindings" budget.
  - R1 to R18 stay within the rules.

### Lane B (zero allocation)

- **What goes:** the heap allocations of arming and firing events (1.4). Each one can wait on the process heap's lock, which the `log-writer`, the executor and the watcher threads share. That is a hidden lock on the key path today. UNVERIFIED as contention, but it is a lock by construction.
- **No new** Win32 call, wake-up or timer change.
- **Risk: the `Decision` copy.** `Decision` is returned by value. If it becomes a fixed-capacity inline array, every event copies it, and that costs a few ns against R1's 83 ns mean.
  - The cut sizes it: handles of 8 bytes rather than `BindAction` values, which are about 56 bytes (UNVERIFIED).
  - Or the cut uses recycled buffers instead (fork T2).
- **Expected bench:**
  - R2, R3, R4 (F13 fires `DoNothing`, so it allocates today), R5 and R6 equal or better.
  - R1, R9 to R12 and R12m within the rules.
- **New evidence row R20:** `Engine::on_key` of a key-down that fires `Launch` (a heap clone today), and of one that arms a Tap+Hold+DoubleTap group, on P and H.
- **Note for the cut.** A counting `#[global_allocator]` in the library's test build is also present in the release bench binary, which is the same harness. With zero allocations measured per event on H it does not change the timed path, but the cut should state that.

## 8. Questions

### For the owner (values)

**Q-3.4-1 (lane A). How should auto-repeats behave once the keys or the window change during a held press?** (known issue 73)

- **Today:** every repeat elects a winner again (`mod.rs:770-774`), while suppression follows the key-down (`mod.rs:920-948`, KS-06). The row-73 example shows the result, pinned by `scenarios.rs:5214-5248`:
  - the config has `J → PressKey("Down")` with `allow_extra_modifiers` and `Ctrl+J → SwitchLanguage` on `Tap(200)` with `block_default: false`;
  - the user holds Ctrl+J and releases Ctrl;
  - the application then gets both `j` repeats and Down at the repeat rate, an output neither binding asks for.
- **Options:**
  - **(a) Lock the winner for the whole press.** The binding that won the key-down owns every repeat until the key-up or a reload, also after a modifier is released or the focus changes.
  - **(b) Keep today's behaviour,** and document it in known issue 73 as by design.
  - **(c) Lock, but stop.** Repeats fire only the press's winner, and only while its keys and `when:` still match.
- **Recommendation: (a).**
  - It aligns firing with suppression: repeats already keep the key-down's suppression across modifier releases and focus changes (rule L, KS-06).
  - It removes the double output.
  - It is the choice Kaleidoscope made for layer keys, so that an arrow under a layer key released a little early keeps repeating (source 2, another project, given as design evidence only).
  - With the lock keyed to the bindings generation, a reload ends it, so `reload_mid_hold_applies_the_new_repeat_flag_from_the_next_repeat` (`scenarios.rs:2990`) stays green.
  - The cost: a held `Shift+H → PressKey(Home)` keeps sending Home after Shift is released. Under (c) it would stop. Under (b), `H`'s own binding would take over.

### Technical forks, decided here with evidence (the architect may overrule)

- **T1. Action storage.** Keep `Arc<BindAction>` in `CompiledBindings`, and let `Decision::fire`, `submit` and the executor's `Job` carry the `Arc`.
  - Why: the executor and `submit` keep owned values; no `ArcSwap` guard is held across `carry_out`, whose comment says the snapshot is dropped before any action runs (`keyboard_hook_callback.rs:258-259`); and `on_timer` keeps its signature (`mod.rs:576`).
  - Cost: one atomic increment per fire. That is not an allocation.
  - Rejected: index references, because the executor would have to resolve them, or clone.
- **T2. `Decision` buffers.** Use inline fixed capacity, sized from a worst case computed at compile.
  - Fires: at most one `Immediate`, one gesture fire, the waiting Taps of other keys, the sequences completing, and at most one chord, since overlapping chord keys are a load error.
  - Timers: at most two, plus the number of sequence bindings.
  - Overflow spills to the heap and is counted. Recycled `Vec`s would add a second `with_main` per firing event to hand the buffer back.
- **T3. The counting allocator.** Put it in the library's `cfg(test)` build, with a thread-local count armed only around the measured calls. Then `engine::testing::Scenario` and `config::from_ron_str`, both `pub(crate)`, can drive it. An integration test would see only the public API.
- **T4. Compile on the loading thread.** The startup thread or `config-watcher` compiles; `MainState::reload` swaps and resets.
- **T5. Diagnostics live in the loader,** next to `duplicate_warnings`, because the messages name keys as the file writes them.
- **T6. Counts.** `swch status` and the reload line keep counting user bindings plus guards.
- **T7. What "10,000 scenarios" counts.** It means scripts checked for press symmetry (Strict plus FocusWhole). Raise their seeds from 64 to about 556 each over the 9 configs, which gives 10,008 scripts. The lane measures the run time of `engine::properties` on the parent and on HEAD; that cost is UNVERIFIED.
- **T8. Generation mismatch.** `on_key` checks the bindings generation against the one the engine was last reset with. A mismatch is treated as a reload, with a `debug_assert`, as a defence for callers that swap without a reset, such as the bench and tests.
- **T9. Moving `BindAction` into `data`:** defer (F8).
- **T10. The suppression-trap key set.**
  - A `chord`, or a `sequence` whose first step, holds letters, digits, OEM punctuation, Space, Enter, Tab or Backspace with no modifier in that chord or step.
  - Also, inferred and to be verified by the architect: a `DoubleTap` group without a Tap whose key-down is swallowed. A single press of such a key fires nothing and loses the key.
- **T11. The shared helper's coverage.** It covers the main loop and the executor. The watchdog keeps its ranged pump (`watchdog/mod.rs:806-826`) and may use the shared wait primitive with its own timeout and mask.
